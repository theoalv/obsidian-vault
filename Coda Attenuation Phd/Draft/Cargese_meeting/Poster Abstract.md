Seismic attenuation provides important constraints on seismic hazard study and small-scale heterogeneity of the lithosphere. It divided to two different mechanism: intristic attenuation, which represent the energy lost through anelastic processes, and scattering attenuation, which elastically redistributes seismic energy due to small-scale structural heterogeneities. Separating these contributions can provide information on both the medium properties and textural complexity of the crust.
Separation of intrinsic and scattering attenuation have traditionally derived from earthquake or active-source observations. However, their spatial coverage is limited by the distribution and occurence of seismic events, which can be particularly limited in regions with sparse earthquake activity.
