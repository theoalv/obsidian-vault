Seismic attenuation provides important constraints on physical properties and small-scale heterogeneity of the lithosphere. It can divided into two different mechanism: intrinsic attenuation, which represent the lost of seismic energy  through anelastic processes, and scattering attenuation, which elastically redistributes seismic energy due to small-scale structural heterogeneities. Separating these contributions can provide information on both the medium properties and textural complexity of the crust.
Separation of intrinsic and scattering attenuation have traditionally derived from earthquake or active-source observations. However, their spatial coverage is limited by the distribution and occurence of seismic events, which can be particularly restrictive in regions with sparse earthquake activity. Recent developments have demostrated that attenuation parameters separation can also be conducted from ambient-noise cross-correlation, providing a passive and continously available alternative.
In this study, we investigate the feasibility of extending ambient-noise analysis to the circum-Adriatic region using the dense AdriaArray seismic network. Ambient-noise data are analyzed through the energy envelopes form their cross-correlation functions. The observed envelopes are modeled using the radiative transfer equation. Intrinsic attenuation is estimated from the late-coda decay, whereas scattering is constrained from the redistribution of energy between the ballistic and early diffuse regimes.
The circum-Adriatic region provides an ideal setting for testing this approach because scattering and intrinsic attenuation distribution of this region have previously been estimated from local earthquake data. The ambient noise derived result can be directly compared with earthquake-based measurements. This comparison will evaluate the consistency between the two approaches and assess whether dense ambient-noise observations can provide a scalable framework for mapping intrinsic and scattering attenuation across large and tectonically complex regions.
