2.1 Tectonic setting
The Circum-Adriatic region represents a highly dynamic belt located in the heart of south-central Europe. This region is one of the most tectonically complex region in Europe due to the convergence between Africa, Adria, and Eurasia [picardi2011]. The quarternary Circum-Adriatic region can be divided into several tectonic domains. The Adriatic Plate is a continental block that acts as the shared foreland for sorrounding orogenic belts[menicheli2025,Oreccio2023]. It has a thickness of 100-120 km, which is thinner than the adjacent European lithosphere[piromallo2003,facenna2014]. 

Along the northern margin of the Adria plate, the Alps form a highly elevated, double-vergent collisional orogenic belt with generally E-W orientation. The orogen is composed of a complex stacking of the European Variscan crystalline basement and its Permo-Mesozoic sedimentary cover, juxtaposed against Adria-derived units. The Alps formed during the Cretaceous to Eocene and Oligocene continent-continent collision, representing the northward indentation of the Adria plate into the European margin after the complete closure of the Alpine Tethys Ocean [carmiati2012, elsharkawy2020].

In the western margin of the Adria plate, lies the Apennines, which an accretionary wedge formed by subduction and delamination of Adriatic lithosphere[carmiati2012]. This wedge is composed of slices of Mesozoic to Cenozoic sedimentary cover sequences that have been scraped off the downgoing plate and stacked, decoupled from the underlying Variscan basement [facenna214]. The Apennines are characterized by a highly active, paired tectonic regime, exhibiting SW-NE oriented across-belt extension and normal faulting along the axial ridge, and coeval compression and shortening along the buried frontal thrusts in the Po Plain and Adriatic offshore [salimbeni2022]. The deep structure is marked by a prominent slab window at 70 - 200 km depth beneath central-southern Apennines where the slab has detached, while beneath the northern Apennines, the slab remains attached[elsharkawy2020].

In the northeastern margin of Adriatic plates, The Dinarides lies with fold and thrust belt. The external portions of the belt are dominated by exceptionally thick Mesozoic to Paleozoic shallow-marine carbonate platform rocks deposited on a crystalline basement[Oreccio2023, facenna2014].  In contrast to the Apennines, the Dinarides did not undergo widespread slab rollback; instead, they experienced a flat subduction characterized by the underplating of continental lithospheric material.[pearce2012] The Albanides represent a fold-and-thrust system in Albania, representing the direct structural continuation of the Dinarides. They are separated by the Skhoder-Peja Normal Fault (SPNF), which cuts directly across the fold-and-thrust belt and overprints the older transverse zone to accommodate active, scissor-like orogen-parallel extension[handy2019]. The external portions of the belt are dominated by exceptionally thick Mesozoic to Paleozoic shallow-marine carbonate platform rocks deposited on a crystalline basement[]. At depth, the system is dominated by deep crustal underplating that has built crustal-scale ramps, driving a pronounced late Miocene-Pliocene exhumation pulse of approximately 3–4 km in the hinterland.

The Hellenides represent the highly active, NW-SE trending subduction-collision system at the southeastern margin of Adria, extending from the central Adriatic Sea to Crete [kiraly2018]. The external belt consists of thick Mesozoic carbonate platform and basinal units, while the internal zones expose Paleozoic basement and ophiolitic fragments [facenna2010]. The Hellenides formed by ongoing subduction of the African and Ionian lithosphere beneath Eurasia since the Eocene-Oligocene, a process accompanied by clockwise oroclinal bending of approximately 50° driven by slab retreat [tsanakas2025,menicheli2025]. The subduction zone is bounded to the northwest by the dextral Kefalonia Transform Fault, which acts as a proto-STEP fault separating continental (Apulia) subduction in the north from oceanic subduction in the south [bird2003,kiraly2018].


2.2. Volcanism 
Active magmatism and volcanism in the Circum-Adriatic region are highly localized, restricted to domains of active subduction rollback or lithospheric rifting. There are two zones with active volcanism, Apennines and Hellenic. The Italian Volcanic province consist of : The Aeolian Arc, volcanoes such as Stromboli and Vulcano produce calc-alkaline magmas representing active subduction and rollback of the Ionian oceanic slab beneath the Calabrian arc; The Roman Volcanic Province with systems like Vesuvius and Campi Flegrei exhibit ultrapotassic to potassium-rich magmatism, driven by melting of a mantle wedge heavily hydrated by fluids released from the subducting slab; The Anorogenic Volcanism with Mt. Etna and the volcanic centers of the Sicily Channel (Pantelleria, Linosa) exhibit alkaline to tholeiitic compositions, which are driven by lithospheric extension, rifting, and asthenospheric mantle upwelling through transtensional tears along slab edges [facenna2014, sambelini2022].

The Hellenic Volcanic Arc has active volcanism is concentrated along the South Aegean Volcanic Arc (Santorini, Milos, Nisyros, Methana), producing calc-alkaline lavas and pyroclastics driven by the northeastward subduction of the dense, old Ionian oceanic lithosphere. In northeastern Greece and Aegean islands, Oligocene-to-Pleistocene volcanism hosted extensive epithermal mineralizing systems, which generated colloform-crustiform amethyst-bearing veins from near-neutral to alkaline fluids .

Orogens like the Alps, Northern Apennines, and Dinarides are primarily accretionary and collisional wedges. They grow by scraping off and stacking highly buoyant, non-volcanic continental basement slices and Mesozoic carbonate platforms. Because subduction has slowed down or ceased beneath these sectors (due to slab break-off or continental collision), there is no flux of subduction fluids to trigger mantle melting, rendering these mountain chains tectonically dry and non-volcanic [orecchio2023]
