High-resolution seismic tomography has provided increasingly detailed constraints on the crustal and lithospheric structure of the Circum-Adriatic region. Body-wave and surface-wave tomography beneath the Adriatic plate revealed marked differences between its northern and southern sectors \citep{venisti_body_2005}. Teleseismic P-wave tomography further showed that these contrasting domains reflect distinct lithospheric structures and tectonic histories: beneath northern Adria, an east-dipping Alpine slab has been associated with continental collision and subsequent delamination, whereas beneath central and southern Adria, slab detachment has been proposed \citep{giacomuzzi_p_2010}. Similar along-strike variations have been identified in the Hellenides, where body-wave tomography distinguishes a relatively thick continental crust beneath the northern Hellenides from thinner oceanic crust beneath the southern sector \citep{pearce_body_2012}. At the crustal scale, ambient-noise and earthquake-derived shear-wave tomography beneath the Alpine collision zone has resolved sedimentary basins, mid-crustal heterogeneities, and variations in crustal thickness, providing a high-resolution reference model for the Alpine crust \citep{kastle_ambient_2018}. In the northern Dinarides and southwestern Pannonian Basin, P-wave tomography similarly reveals pronounced crustal thickening beneath the Dinarides and crustal thinning towards the Pannonian Basin, with a distinct velocity increase at approximately 20 km depth suggesting a layered crustal structure \citep{kapuralic_p_2018}. More recent Vp and Vp/Vs tomography of Adria has further highlighted the contrasting modes of continental subduction along its margins, with slab rollback and steep subduction beneath the Apennines contrasting with underplating and relatively flat subduction beneath the Dinarides \citep{menichelli_2025}. Together, these studies demonstrate substantial lateral variations in crustal and lithospheric structure across Adria and its surrounding orogens.

Several high-resolution seismic tomography studies have increasingly constrained the crustal and lithospheric structure of the Circum-Adriatic region. Body- and surface-wave tomography beneath the Adriatic plate revealed distinct seismic characteristics between its northern and southern sectors \citep{venisti_tomographic_2005}. Across the surrounding orogens, tomography has resolved pronounced crustal heterogeneities, including sedimentary basins, mid-crustal anomalies, and crustal-thickness variations beneath the Alps and Apennines \citep{kastle_surface_2018}, a thickened and layered crust beneath the northern Dinarides contrasting with crustal thinning beneath the Pannonian Basin \citep{kapuralic_crustal_2019}, and contrasting crustal structures associated with the two-sided subduction of Adria beneath the Apennines and Dinarides \citep{menichelli_two-sided_2025}. Recent P-wave tomography further provides a regional crustal model spanning the Alps, northern Adria, and northern Dinarides \citep{bagagli}. Similar lateral variations occur farther southeast, where body-wave tomography beneath the western Hellenides identified a thick continental crust beneath the northern Hellenides and a much thinner, oceanic crust beneath the southern sector \citep{pearce_seismic_2012}. At upper-mantle depths, teleseismic and surface-wave tomography has revealed contrasting lithospheric structures around Adria, including Alpine and Apennine subduction, detached and delaminated Adriatic lithosphere, and possible lithospheric thinning and mantle upwelling beneath the Dinarides and Pannonian Basin \citep{giacomuzzi_linking_2011,koulakov,elsharkawy_slab_2020,belinic_shear-wave_2021,paffrath,handy_orogenic_2021}. Together, these studies highlight the strong lateral heterogeneity and complex subduction, slab-detachment, and mantle processes that characterize the Circum-Adriatic region.
