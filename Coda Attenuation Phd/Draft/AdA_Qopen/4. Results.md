intrinsic (Qi)
Shown a relatively similar pattern for all frequency. Apennines shown a high attenuation especially in the western area, where volcanic arc. The alps shown low intrinsic attenuation. Dinarides has low intrinsic, but become a little higher at higher freq. Dinarides has med-high attenuation, which become stronger as frequency increases. The high intrinsic zone in hellenides in low frequency concentrated along the subduction zone along the margin, while in higher frequency (above 6 hz cf) the central zone nearby agaean sea and nearby volcanic arc has higher attenuation which was previously low in lower central freq. notable high attenuation in high frequency (above 6) is also shown in east of dinarides, nearby pannonian basin.
Scattering (Qsc)
At low cf, high scattering observed in central and south apennines, especially where the volcanic arc. Northern apennines and alps has low scattering attenuation. dinarides also shown low scattering. strong scattering observed in the albanides and hellenides only along the tectonic boundary. there is also a high scattering in some part of pannonian basin and eastern part of moessian basin. at high cf, (more than 6 hz) the pattern chaged. some of the region in apennines near the volcano still have high scattering. northern apeninnes near Po basin and some part of eastern alps also has high scattering. dinarides shown a high scattering and also albanides. In hellenides, the region near volcanic arc in agaean sea has high scattering, and also eastern part that  near turkey. The region in the Bulgaria, south of moessian basin also have noticeable high scattering.

Sample of coda envelope in different zone
Figure x showed the coda envelope from different zones. All earthquakes picked with similar depth around 10 km and magnitude ~3, and showed 6 stations with different R (from close ~15km to far ~100 km from event).
Event in Apennines generally showed a strong coda energy envelope with strongly decreased S bulk energy as distance increased, which indicates a strong scattering. At short distance, the envelope showed a strong S wave energy spike, but as distance increase, the spike seems flatter because the S wave energy decreased and coda energy behind it increased. At some distance (around 60 km in most apennines), the spike is gone and the s wave to coda has a smooth transition, which means they has similar energy level, or most of the energy from the s wave has been scattered and transformed into coda. This is expected because there are many volcanoes in Apennines which usually cause high crustal heterogeneity.
Similar envelope pattern was observed in Po Basin, this may be caused by thick sediment in this area which may cause small scale heterogeneity.
In alps, s wave energy spikes remain strong even at a long distance (more than 80 km), even in some cases, it still has strong spikes at station ~100km. This suggested that most of the energy in S wave is not scattered and stay as S wave, and coda energy is also low, shown by fast envelope decay after S wave bulk, even at long station distance. This is expected as alps known for no heterogeneity in small scale which may cause scattering.
Event in dinarides show similar coda envelope in alps, but with different scale. The S bulk at long distance is still shown a spike, although not as strong as in Alps. This suggest that Dinarides has low scattering shown by low coda energy.
The envelope pattern in albanides and hellenides shown a strong coda energy at long station distance (more than 70 km). Which suggest that this region has higher scattering.
