The Circum-Adriatic region represents a highly dynamic belt located in the heart of south-central Europe. This region is one of the most tectonically complex region in Europe due to the convergence between Africa, Adria, and Eurasia [ref].
The quarternary Circum-Adriatic region can be divided into several tectonic domains. The Adriatic Plate is a continental block that acts as the shared foreland for sorrounding orogenic belts. It has a thickness of 100-120 km, which is thinner than the adjacent European lithosphere. Along the northern margin of the Adria plate, the Alps form a highly elevated, double-vergent collisional orogenic belt.
In the western margin, lies the Apennines, which an accretionary wedge formed by subduction and delamination of Adriatic lithosphere. This wedge is composed of slices of Mesozoic to Cenozoic sedimentary cover sequences that have been scraped off the downgoing plate and stacked, decoupled from the underlying Variscan basement along a regional Triassic evaporite décollement[ref]. The Apennines are characterized by a highly active, paired tectonic regime, exhibiting SW-NE oriented across-belt extension and normal faulting along the axial ridge, and coeval compression and shortening along the buried frontal thrusts in the Po Plain and Adriatic offshore [ref]. The deeo structure is marked by a prominent slab window at 70 - 200 km depth beneath central-southern Apennines where the slab has detached, while beneath the northern Apennines, the slab remains attached[ref].
In the northeastern margin of Adriatic plates, The Dinarides lies with fold and thrust belt. The external portions of the belt are dominated by exceptionally thick Mesozoic to Paleozoic shallow-marine carbonate platform rocks deposited on a Variscan crystalline basement[ref].  In contrast to the Apennines, the Dinarides did not undergo widespread slab rollback; instead, they experienced a **flat subduction style** characterized by the underplating of buoyant continental lithospheric material
