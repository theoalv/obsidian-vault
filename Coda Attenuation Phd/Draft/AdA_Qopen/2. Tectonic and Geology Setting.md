The Circum-Adriatic region represents a highly dynamic belt located in the heart of south-central Europe. This region is one of the most tectonically complex region in Europe due to the convergence between Africa, Adria, and Eurasia [ref].
The quarternary Circum-Adriatic region can be divided into several tectonic domains. The Adriatic Plate is a continental block that acts as the shared foreland for sorrounding orogenic belts. It has a thickness of 100-120 km, which is thinner than the adjacent European lithosphere. In the western margin, lies the Apennines, which an accretionary wedge formed by subduction and delamination of Adriatic lithosphere. The Apennines has a paired tectonic regime, where the axial ridge is an active extensional normal faulting, while the frontal arc has an compression thrust, expanded to burried frontal trust in the Po Plain and Adriatic sea [ref].
