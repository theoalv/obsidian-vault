The Albanides form the direct structural continuation of the Dinarides, sharing their NW–SE trend and SW-directed vergence, but are separated from them by the Shkoder–Peja Normal Fault (SPNF). The SPNF overprints an older transverse structural zone and accommodates active, scissor-like orogen-parallel extension, marking the transition from the predominantly compressional Dinarides to the extensional Albanides–Hellenides system [Handy et al., 2019]. Although the Albanides have historically been considered part of the Northern Hellenides, we distinguish them here because they are underlain by continental crust, in contrast to the oceanic crust of the Southern Hellenides [Pearce et al., 2012], and share a closer structural and kinematic affinity with the Dinarides. The Albanides accommodate the eastward subduction of the Adriatic continental lithosphere, with recent work suggesting that their growth involved deep-crustal underplating, whereby slices of Adriatic continental crust were detached from the downgoing plate and accreted beneath the shallow sedimentary cover [Rossetti et al., 2024]. Present-day deformation is relatively low (<2 mm yr$^{-1}$), comparable to that of the Dinarides, despite the contrasting extensional regime south of the SPNF.
